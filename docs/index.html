The Terminal List

I'll help anyone. Everyone. Almost anyone.

Help is the default. Low on health with three adds? Somebody steps in. The Terminal List is a short file of the players who spent that goodwill in one go, so the next time one of them is face-down under five mobs, you already know: keep walking, save your mana for somebody who'd do the same for you.

01Rules of Engagement

The doctrine, such as it is. Read it before you add anyone.

MEMO // ROE-001RE: Why this exists

I help people. That's the setting it shipped with. You're losing to a pack of wolves, I'll drop what I'm doing. You died in a cave, I'll walk your corpse run with you if I'm nearby.

But some players make it very clear, very fast, that they'd never return the favour. They take the ore out from under you, the chest you waited on, the kill you earned, and then they ride off.

Those players go on The List. Once they're on it I don't have to think about it again. When I find one of them later, pinned by five mobs and yelling for help, I check the crosshair, I wave, and I keep my energy for somebody who deserves it.

Nobody's being hunted. Help stays free. These folks just gave theirs up.

— The Management. Filed in a good mood, mostly.

  1. Help is the default.Heal the stranger. Peel the add. Res the guy on the road. Nobody pays for that.
  2. You have to earn your spot.One wipe, one bad pull or one clumsy moment isn't a crime. Doing it on purpose is.
  3. The file stays open.Your entry doesn't expire. Change your attitude and you can explain it to the five murlocs.
  4. Spend your energy wisely.Mana, potions and time are limited. Use them on players who'd give some back.
  5. The List remembers. It doesn't attack.It's a watch list, not a hit list. It marks people; what you do next is up to you, and the answer is usually "nothing."

03Capability

What the addon actually does. No exaggeration; it's v0.1.0 and in early in-game testing.

The list

A personal watch list for players who crossed you, were rude, or just don't play nice. Both factions.

Account-wide: add someone on one character and every character on the account sees it. Stored only on your computer, in your saved variables. Nothing is uploaded or shared.

Adding by hand

/tlist add Name note, or target them and type /tlist add. Notes say why they're on it.

Right-click a player's portrait or their name in chat: Add to The Terminal List, or Edit note / Remove for someone already listed.

Open-world PvP kills

If a player kills you in the open world, they're added for you, and their kill count goes up on repeats.

Never counted: battlegrounds, arenas, dungeons, raids, any other instance, and duels. That's PvP you signed up for.

Recognition

A red crosshair (not a skull) beside their nameplate and on the target frame. A tooltip line with your note and kill count. A crosshair on their chat lines, and their name in red when someone mentions them.

An optional once-per-session alert when you first spot one. Off by default.

The window

/tlist, the minimap button or the addon compartment opens the list: name-realm, race, class, faction, note, how they were added, kills, date and zone.

Search, filter by PvP or manual, sort by newest, most kills or name, edit notes, remove.

Intel gap: the combat log on Forever

WoW Forever may not give addons the combat log, and without it no addon can see who landed the killing blow. If that's the case on your client, The Terminal List doesn't guess. After an open-world death it asks: "You were fighting [your hostile target]. Add them?" You decide.

When the combat log is available, killers are added automatically. That path is written and tested against a mock, but it hasn't been confirmed in game yet.

CommandWhat it does
/tlistOpen or close the window
/tlist add [Name[-Realm]] [note]Add a player; no name means your current target
/tlist remove [Name[-Realm]]Remove; no name means your current target
/tlist note [Name[-Realm]] [text]Set the reason note (no text opens an editor)
/tlist search <text>Filter by name, realm, note, zone, race or class
/tlist settingsSettings
/tlist helpThe commands

04Acquire the Package

WoW Forever only. Free, open source, MIT. No keybinds, no macros, no Ace3.

OmaForge (Linux)
$ omaforge install -c forever github:ninepointlabs/the-terminal-list

By hand (any OS): download the zip from the v0.1.0 release (or all releases) and unzip it so the folder is _forever_/Interface/AddOns/TheTerminalList.

Then /reload and /tlist. Turn on player nameplates in the game's options so the crosshair has somewhere to go.

Source, issues and the in-game test checklist: github.com/ninepointlabs/the-terminal-list.
